Staff your hospitality business with an AI co-pilot — then prove you know better

You're the HR Manager (or hiring manager) for a hospitality, culinary, pastry, or baking arts business — a restaurant, hotel, bakery, café, catering company, resort, or your own dream concept. Invent a role you need to fill. Instead of writing the job description alone, you'll draft it with Microsoft Copilot, then use what you learned in Chapter 6 to catch what it got wrong and make it right. Hospitality hiring has its own realities — split shifts, holidays, physical demands, food safety, guest-facing standards — and your final version needs to reflect them. The final portfolio is graded as one submission with seven parts.

Need a role to hire for? Try Pastry Chef, Bakery Production Lead, Cake Decorator, Sous Chef, Line Cook, Banquet Captain, Front Desk Supervisor, Restaurant General Manager, Barista Trainer, Catering Coordinator, Beverage Manager, Housekeeping Supervisor, or Events Sales Manager. Or invent your own: a night-shift bread baker for a new artisan bakery, a seasonal pastry team for a holiday resort, a guest experience lead for a boutique hotel.
Tool for this assignment: Microsoft Copilot
Use your FEI Microsoft 365 login at copilot.microsoft.com. Signing in with your school account keeps this work under FEI's data protections — do not paste real people's private information into any AI tool.

How the seven parts fit together

  1. A. Sources — find 3 real hospitality, culinary, or baking job postings for a role like the one you're inventing.
  2. B. Prompt — build one detailed prompt using those postings as reference material.
  3. C. Draft 1 — paste Copilot's first output in full, completely unedited.
  4. D. Annotate — mark up that draft against Chapter 6: what's generic, biased, vague, or legally risky for a hospitality workplace?
  5. E. Final version — your human-edited job description and person specification.
  6. F. Reflection — what AI got right, what it missed, and your AI-use disclosure.
  7. G. Download & submit — download your finished portfolio as a PDF and upload it to the Canvas submission page.
Draft 1 must stay unedited. Part of what's being graded is your ability to spot problems in AI output — if you clean it up before pasting it in, there's nothing left for you to catch in Part D.

Part A

Find your 3 real hospitality job postings

Search a site like Indeed, LinkedIn, Hcareers, Culinary Agents, Poached, or a restaurant, hotel, or bakery's own careers page for a role similar to the one you plan to invent. Paste the link to each posting below — these become the material Copilot will draw from in Part B.

Tip: pull your postings from three different kinds of employers (for example, a hotel, an independent bakery, and a restaurant group) so Copilot can see how the same role changes across hospitality settings.
Part B

Build your Copilot prompt

A vague prompt gets a generic answer — and "fast-paced environment" appears in nearly every hospitality posting. Fill in the template below with your business and role details (shifts, physical demands, certifications, guest standards), and run it in Copilot before moving on.

"I am hiring for [job title] at [type of hospitality business, e.g. boutique hotel / artisan bakery / full-service restaurant / catering company]. Here are 3 real job postings for comparable roles: [paste the links to all 3 postings]. Using these as reference, write a job description that includes: job title, responsibilities, required skills/qualifications (including any food safety or industry certifications), work environment (e.g. kitchen, bakery production floor, front of house, hotel property), schedule expectations (early mornings, nights, weekends, holidays), physical demands, and any unique aspects of the role (tips, staff meals, seasonal peaks, training). Then write a person specification listing essential and desirable attributes, including soft skills, guest-service mindset, teamwork under pressure, and cultural fit considerations. Do not just copy the postings — synthesize the best elements and make it specific to [my business concept and location]."

Part C

Draft 1 — the raw Copilot output

Paste Copilot's response exactly as it appeared. No trimming, fixing typos, or reordering — you'll do your editing in Part E.
Part D

Annotate Draft 1 against Chapter 6

Go line by line. Use these four lenses to find what Copilot got wrong or left generic — and remember that hospitality workplaces have their own legal and safety realities.

Any language that could raise ADA, EEOC, or age/gender-bias concerns? Think about physical requirements (lifting, standing, heat), minimum ages for alcohol service or kitchen equipment, overtime and tipped-wage rules, and food safety and allergen requirements.
Gendered job titles (waitress, hostess), "young and energetic" wording, appearance or accent requirements, "work hard, play hard" kitchen-culture red flags, or requirements that unnecessarily exclude qualified candidates?
Where does the draft say things like "fast-paced environment" or "passion for food" instead of real, measurable requirements (for example, covers per service, batches per shift, certifications, guest-satisfaction standards)?
Does the person specification actually match the responsibilities listed, per Ch.6 theory? (For example, does a line cook role really require a four-year degree, or the skills the job actually uses?)
Part E

Your final, human-edited version

Rewrite using what you found in Part D. This is the version you'd actually be proud to post on a hospitality job board.

Part F

Reflection & AI-use disclosure

2–4 sentences: what was Copilot genuinely useful for, what did it miss about hospitality realities (shifts, physical demands, food safety, guest standards), and how did your HR knowledge change the outcome?
